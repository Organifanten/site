Most teams either fight too much or avoid conflict entirely. Both patterns are expensive. This retrospective asks you to look at your actual conflict history and extract the useful parts. Building on the feedback skills from Activities 21-22, you're now examining how disagreement itself can become a tool.

## Theory

Discuss what conditions foster constructive disagreement. Connect these insights to your charter. This turns conflicts into learning moments rather than threats.

Conflict is not the problem — its unspeakability is. When teams avoid disagreement, resentment builds underground and surfaces as passive-aggression or quiet withdrawal. The biographical work from Activity 31 gave you the vocabulary to understand where your conflict patterns come from; now you examine how those patterns play out in this team and what conditions allow disagreement to improve decisions rather than damage relationships. Connecting these insights to the values you committed to in your team charter makes the abstract concrete. Small, consistently kept agreements about how you handle friction change culture more effectively than grand protocols.
