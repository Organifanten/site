You don't bring a neutral self to team conflict. You bring years of experience — family patterns, past teams, cultural defaults, and wounds that never fully healed. Naming that background makes you predictable to yourself and understandable to others. The psychological safety from Levels 1-4 is what makes this sharing possible without it feeling reckless.

## Theory

Biographical conversations build understanding for differences. Prioritize trust and voluntariness. You'll learn to take conflicts less personally.

Conflict behavior is rooted in biography — the rules you absorbed about disagreement long before this team existed. These adaptations are not character flaws but survival strategies, and making them visible to teammates is a structured intervention that reduces the personal charge of future disputes. This exercise requires the trust you built through Levels 1-4; without that foundation, sharing conflict histories feels reckless rather than productive. The goal is not to eliminate conflict but to slow it down at the right moment, so you can distinguish between the pattern you are running and what the situation actually requires.
