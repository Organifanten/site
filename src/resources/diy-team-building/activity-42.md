Most teams recognize each other for output. This lesson asks for something harder and more meaningful: naming the human qualities you value in someone, not just their contributions. It closes a loop that started with the early appreciation exercises and deepens it with everything the team has learned about each other since.

## Theory

By Level 12, you have spent real time with these people. You have seen them under pressure, in conflict, in joy, and in the quiet work of showing up consistently. That knowledge is rare and worth naming. The exercise asks each person to write or say something specific about each teammate. Not generic praise. Not "you are great." Something grounded in what you have actually witnessed. The gratitude practice from Activity 16 and the team identity work from Activities 25 through 28 created the foundation for this. But this goes deeper because it is personal, not procedural. When someone tells you what they value about you, and it is specific and true, it changes the relationship. It becomes harder to reduce a person to their role or their output. That is exactly the point.
