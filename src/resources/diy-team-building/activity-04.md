A team without a clear purpose drifts. People pull in different directions and wonder why progress feels slow. Defining why you exist together gives every future decision a reference point. It does not need to be poetic. It needs to be honest.

## Theory

Define your mission and purpose together. A clear "why" creates direction and motivation. Write the result somewhere visible as a foundation for later decisions.

A team charter begins with purpose because directionless collaboration exhausts people. After three activities of integration — getting to know each other, finding common ground — you now have enough shared understanding to tackle the bigger question of why you exist as a team. This is the first structural element of your charter, and it works because it is built on relationships, not imposed from above. You are still building Level 2 relationships, and a shared mission gives that trust a direction. Division of labor creates inherent tension around fairness and visibility; a shared purpose gives those trade-offs a reference point.
