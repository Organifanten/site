# Intervention Catalogue

A catalogue of organizational interventions.

| Org. Component | Actions                                      |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| People         | Hire, train, convene, reorganize, remove     |
| Technologies   | Explore, integrate, diffuse, upgrade, remove |
| Processes      | Create, refine, remove                       |
| Policies       | Define, refine, remove                       |
| Artifacts      | Create, refactor, standardize, remove        |

## People

The humans in the organization. Who is here, what they can do, and how they are arranged.

### Hire people

Bring someone new into the organization. Hiring changes the composition of the team and introduces new capabilities, perspectives, or relationships.

### Train people

Develop the capabilities of existing people. Training changes what people can do, not who they are or where they sit.

### Convene people

Bring people together for a structured purpose. A sensemaking session, a workshop, a retrospective — all convene actions. They create temporary structures that dissolve once the purpose is met.

### Reorganize people

Change how people are allocated across teams, roles, or functions. Reorganizing changes the permanent structure, not a temporary assembly.

### Remove people

Take someone out of the organization. Removal changes composition by subtraction.

## Technologies

The tools and systems the organization uses to get work done.

### Explore technology

Evaluate a new technology before committing to it. Exploration happens before integration — you are learning, not adopting.

### Integrate technology

Connect a new technology into existing workflows. Integration is the moment technology goes from "being evaluated" to "being used."

### Diffuse technology

Spread technology adoption across teams. A tool might be integrated in one team and unknown in another. Diffusion closes that gap.

### Upgrade technology

Improve existing technology. Upgrading changes what a tool can do without replacing it.

### Remove technology

Decommission technology that is no longer needed. Removal eliminates a tool from the organization's stack.

## Processes

How work flows — the steps, handoffs, and sequences that turn input into output.

### Create process

Establish a new process where none existed. Creation brings structure to previously unstructured work.

### Refine process

Adjust an existing process. Refinement changes how a process works without replacing it entirely.

### Remove process

Stop doing a process. Removal eliminates steps, handoffs, or sequences that no longer serve a purpose.

## Policies

What is allowed, rewarded, and decided. The rules that shape behavior without requiring constant oversight.

### Define policy

Establish a new policy. Definition creates a rule where none existed — who decides what, what is permitted, what gets rewarded.

### Refine policy

Adjust an existing policy. Refinement changes the rules without removing them.

### Remove policy

Eliminate a policy. Removal takes away a rule that is no longer useful or that actively constrains.

## Artifacts

What the organization has produced and made available — documents, templates, guides, dashboards, knowledge bases.

### Create artifact

Produce a new artifact. Creation makes something available that did not exist before.

### Refactor artifact

Restructure an existing artifact. Refactoring changes how an artifact is organized without changing its purpose.

### Standardize artifact

Make artifacts consistent across the organization. Standardization reduces variation so people encounter the same structure and format everywhere.

### Remove artifact

Eliminate an artifact. Removal takes something out of circulation that is no longer useful or that creates confusion.

---

## Second-order intervention: Change how you do something

E.g. change how you hire, train, convene, reorganize, or remove people. Change how you explore, integrate, diffuse, upgrade, or remove technology. Change how you create, refine, or remove processes. Change how you define, refine, or remove policies. Change how you create, refactor, standardize, or remove artifacts.
