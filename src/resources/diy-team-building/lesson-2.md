      <h2 class="text-2xl font-semibold mt-10 mb-4">The Polarity of Team Development</h2>

      <p>A team constantly moves within a social-psychological tension field. On one side is the need for connection, orientation, and belonging. On the other side is the striving for individuality, distinction, and personal expression.</p>

      <p>Successful team development does not mean resolving these poles. It means finding a <strong>productive way to work with both</strong>. It creates space for integration <em>and</em> differentiation.</p>

      <div class="rounded-xl border border-base-300 p-6 my-8">
        <h3 class="font-semibold mb-2">How the game reflects this</h3>
        <p class="text-sm opacity-80">Levels 1–3 focus heavily on integration: shared goals, team charter, understanding each other. Levels 4–6 introduce differentiation: strengths, roles, healthy competition. The game's structure embodies the principle of "integration before differentiation."</p>
      </div>

      <h2 class="text-2xl font-semibold mt-10 mb-4">Integration: What Holds the Team Together</h2>

      <p>Integration is the connective tissue of a team. It includes shared rules, common values, and collective goals. Integration creates safety and a sense of "we." It is the foundation without which difference is quickly experienced as a threat.</p>

      <p>When team members feel connected, they can handle disagreement, give honest feedback, and challenge each other without damaging relationships. Integration creates the psychological safety needed for productive conflict.</p>

      <h2 class="text-2xl font-semibold mt-10 mb-4">Differentiation: What Makes the Team Stronger</h2>

      <p>Differentiation is the counter-movement. It is the space for individuality, unique strengths, different perspectives, and personal expression. When integration is solid, differentiation becomes a source of:</p>

      <ul class="space-y-2 opacity-80 ml-6 list-disc">
        <li><strong>Creativity</strong> — different viewpoints spark new ideas</li>
        <li><strong>Ownership</strong> — people can contribute in their own way</li>
        <li><strong>Growth</strong> — productive friction pushes the team forward</li>
      </ul>

      <p>Without differentiation, a team becomes a hive mind — harmonious but stagnant, incapable of critical thinking or innovation.</p>

      <h2 class="text-2xl font-semibold mt-10 mb-4">Integration Before Differentiation</h2>

      <p>The sequence matters. A team must have enough shared ground before it can productively explore its differences. This is a developmental principle: before you can distinguish yourselves from each other, you must feel connected to each other.</p>

      <p>Rushing to differentiation without sufficient integration often leads to misunderstanding, conflict, or withdrawal. Team members may feel judged, unheard, or unsafe. The game's levels follow this logic: early levels build connection; later levels create space for difference.</p>

      <div class="border border-warning/30 rounded-xl p-6 my-8">
        <h3 class="font-semibold text-warning mb-2">A note on complexity</h3>
        <p class="text-sm opacity-80">The cause-effect relationship described here — integration leading to shared understanding, differentiation leading to stronger relationships — is a simplification. Real team dynamics are multi-layered and complex. This framing is a deliberate choice: it provides an actionable starting point without getting lost in systemic complexity.</p>
      </div>
