When conflict escalates beyond task or process disagreement, it often reaches the level of values. Two people are not just disagreeing about a decision. They are protecting something that matters deeply to them. Without the ability to see this, teams default to assigning blame or avoiding the topic entirely.

## Theory

Values conflict is the hardest kind because it feels existential. One person values speed because they have learned that hesitation loses opportunity. Another person values thoroughness because they have seen what happens when details are ignored. Neither is wrong. They are protecting different things they care about. The team identity work from Activities 25 through 28 and the values exercises from Activities 27 and 28 laid the groundwork for this. When a team has already named its shared values, it can reference those during conflict instead of reacting from instinct alone. The goal is not to resolve every values clash. Sometimes two legitimate values sit in tension and the team must choose a path while honoring both. What matters is that everyone can name what is at stake. That transforms the conversation from "you are wrong" to "we are navigating something difficult together."
