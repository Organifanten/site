No team of real humans has perfect balance. Someone always pushes for action while another pushes for caution. Someone focuses on ideas while someone else focuses on details. Belbin's team roles give you a shared vocabulary for these differences. The self-awareness from Activities 9-12 and the appreciation skills from Activities 13-16 make this conversation productive rather than threatening.

## Theory

Analyze your team roles: who brings which strengths and tasks? The discussion fosters understanding and balance. Use the insights to design responsibilities more consciously.

Roles and division of labor are the technical structures of a socio-technical system, and when they are ambiguous or hidden, they breed conflict. Belbin's framework gives you a shared vocabulary for differences that might otherwise feel like personal friction — the person who always wants more data is not being difficult but protecting the team from premature decisions. This conversation builds on the self-awareness from earlier activities and connects directly to the charter you developed in Activity 28: knowing your roles makes it possible to assign responsibilities honestly rather than by default. When differences are framed as contributions rather than annoyances, collaboration shifts fundamentally.
