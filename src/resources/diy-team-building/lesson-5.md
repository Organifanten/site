    <div class="rounded-xl border border-base-300 p-6 mb-12">
      <p class="font-medium mb-2">Learning outcome</p>
      <p class="opacity-80">Learn a practical sequence for team development — from the first moment people meet to ongoing practices that keep a team healthy, aligned, and growing.</p>

    <section class="space-y-6 leading-relaxed">

      <p class="text-xl italic opacity-60 border-l-4 border-primary pl-4 py-2">"Team development doesn't start with a grand plan. It starts with the first moment people meet."</p>

      <p>Whoever shapes a team shapes relationships — consciously or unconsciously. The more powerful approach is to actively steer this process. Here are six things you can do, ideally in this order.</p>

      <h2 class="text-2xl font-semibold mt-10 mb-4">1. Conscious Start: Kick-off with Real Encounters</h2>

      <p>At the beginning, one thing matters most: <strong>creating connection</strong>. A good kick-off is more than a project launch. It's a relationship moment. Icebreakers or story-sharing rounds invite people to show more than just their roles — they reveal who they are.</p>

      <p>Who am I? What motivates me? How do I prefer to work? These insights lay the foundation for trust and open conversations about what the team wants to achieve together. A team charter and a clear goal belong at the start — not as formalities, but as expressions of direction and intention.</p>

      <div class="rounded-xl border border-base-300 p-6 my-8">
        <h3 class="font-semibold mb-2">In the game</h3>
        <p class="text-sm opacity-80">Level 1 tasks — walking together, mind maps, finding commonalities, and creating the first team charter — embody this principle. They're designed to create connection before diving into anything harder.</p>
      </div>

      <h2 class="text-2xl font-semibold mt-10 mb-4">2. Cultivate Relationships and Understanding in Daily Work</h2>

      <p>Team development happens "on the job" — in the small things, again and again. In how teams meet each other in meetings. In conversations between door and wall. In moments when someone says: "I see you." Invest in shared time beyond work and in clarifying shared understanding.</p>

      <h2 class="text-2xl font-semibold mt-10 mb-4">3. Workshops as Conscious Development Spaces</h2>

      <p>Sometimes more space is needed for reflection, learning, and new perspectives. Good workshops offer not just methods but <strong>protected spaces</strong> where teams can question and shape their collaboration. Ideally, over the course of its development, a team makes a shared learning experience in each of the structural intervention areas: perception, communication, decisions, roles, and conflict.</p>

      <div class="rounded-xl border border-base-300 p-6 my-8">
        <h3 class="font-semibold mb-2">In the game</h3>
        <p class="text-sm opacity-80">Level 8 includes planning a joint training. The game doesn't prescribe what — it asks the team to choose what they need most. This respects that every team's development path is unique.</p>
      </div>

      <h2 class="text-2xl font-semibold mt-10 mb-4">4. Regularly Renew Team Goal and Charter</h2>

      <p>What was clarified at the start is not set in stone. Teams evolve: goals shift, new members join, others leave. Regularly reflecting on the charter and team goal keeps integration intact. The game reflects this by revisiting the team charter at every second level, each time at a deeper layer.</p>

      <h2 class="text-2xl font-semibold mt-10 mb-4">5. Establish Retrospectives with Relationship Time</h2>

      <p>Retrospectives are more than reviews. They are training for togetherness. When teams run them regularly and expand them — with moments of gratitude or small personal insights — trust grows through repetition. Those who regularly pause recognize not only patterns but also people, developing the capacity to shape tensions together.</p>

      <div class="rounded-xl border border-base-300 p-6 my-8">
        <h3 class="font-semibold mb-2">In the game</h3>
        <p class="text-sm opacity-80">Every even-numbered level (2, 4, 6, 8, 10) includes a retrospective task. Each one focuses on a different question: collaboration, trust, goal progress, conflict, and difficult situations. This keeps reflection as a rhythm, not an event.</p>
      </div>

      <h2 class="text-2xl font-semibold mt-10 mb-4">6. Shape Team Identity Together</h2>

      <p>Teams that take time to talk about their values, language, and self-image gain more than a nice vision statement: they develop a <strong>shared narrative</strong> that carries them. Whether through symbols, rituals, or inside jokes — identity forms where people recognize themselves in each other.</p>

      <div class="rounded-xl border border-base-300 p-6 my-8">
        <h3 class="font-semibold mb-2">In the game</h3>
        <p class="text-sm opacity-80">Level 7 is dedicated entirely to identity: a team photo, a 5-sentence self-portrait, an information radiator, and a charter on what you want to stand for. This is the midpoint of the year — a natural time to look inward as a team.</p>
      </div>

    </section>

    <section class="mt-16 border-t border-base-300 pt-8">
      <h2 class="text-xl font-semibold mb-4">Putting It All Together</h2>
      <p class="opacity-80">Team development is not a linear project with a fixed end date. It's an ongoing practice of paying attention to how people work together. The six steps above form a cycle, not a checklist. Run through them, revisit them, adapt them to your team's rhythm.</p>
      <p class="opacity-80 mt-4">The DIY Team Building game gives you a structure for a year of this practice. The lessons give you the understanding behind it. But the real work — showing up, being curious, staying with the complexity — that's yours to do.</p>
    </section>

        <li>• If you could implement one of these steps this week, which would have the biggest impact?</li>
        <li>• What would it take to make team development a rhythm rather than a one-time event?</li>
