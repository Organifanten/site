Appreciation is cheap when it is spoken in passing and powerful when it is written down. A thank-you note is tangible. It can be held, re-read, and remembered. In Level 2 we explored who each person is. In Level 3 we started making decisions together. Now I pause and name what others have contributed.

## Theory

A handwritten thank-you has more impact than any email. Be specific about what you are grateful for. Notes can be anonymous or open. The key is sincerity.

Kapitel 4 teaches that structured interventions create safe frames for honest exchange, and a thank-you note is one of the simplest such frames. When you articulate what someone did and why it mattered, you produce a concrete record of value — something the recipient can return to long after the moment has passed. Building on the listening skills from Activity 14 and the appreciation habits explored in earlier levels, this practice turns gratitude from a passing comment into a deliberate act. Specificity is what makes it land: not "thanks for being great" but "thanks for catching that error before it reached the client."
