After building trust and shared identity in Levels 1-3, it is time to put that trust into motion. A cooperative game removes the pressure of real work while still demanding real coordination. I get to see how my team actually operates under mild stress, who steps up, who hangs back, and where the group naturally finds its rhythm.

## Theory

Cooperative games foster joint planning and trust. Notice how you communicate and who takes the lead. Discuss briefly afterward what stood out about your teamwork.

Now that you have built trust and shared identity through Activities 1–12, a cooperative game puts that foundation into motion. The low stakes of play create a safe frame for structured intervention: you observe real communication patterns, role adoption, and group rhythm without the pressure of actual deliverables. This is Kapitel 4 in practice — self-reflection and communication exercised through a shared task rather than discussed abstractly. The game becomes a rehearsal space where the interpersonal skills developed across earlier levels surface naturally.
